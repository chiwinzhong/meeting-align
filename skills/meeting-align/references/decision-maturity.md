# Decision maturity

Decision maturity prevents a polished summary from flattening exploration into commitment.

## Ladder

| Level | Meaning | Typical evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `IDEA` | A possibility without meaningful convergence | “Maybe”, “we could”, “another option” |
| `HYPOTHESIS` | A plausible direction worth testing | test, research, validate, conditional reasoning |
| `DIRECTIONAL_CONSENSUS` | Shared direction or boundary without execution-complete choices | agreement on principle while scope, owner, offer, or timing remains open |
| `CONFIRMED_DECISION` | A concrete choice is settled | authorized confirmation, explicit agreement, decisive recap |
| `COMMITTED_ACTION` | A person or team explicitly commits to act | named actor plus commitment language; deadline may still be missing |

## Upgrade rules

1. Assign the highest level supported by the latest evidence.
2. Preserve earlier uncertainty and the upgrade path in evidence pointers.
3. Keep a committed action separate from the decision or direction that motivates it.
4. Mark missing deadline or definition of done as a gap; do not downgrade a genuine commitment merely because fields are incomplete.
5. Do not upgrade on silence, politeness, enthusiasm, repetition, or one participant acting as if agreement exists.

## Mismatch

Use `MATURITY_MISMATCH` when participants treat the same statement at materially different levels. Use `READINESS_MISMATCH` when they share a direction but disagree about whether to build, launch, sell, or operationalize now.

Maturity is not quality. Ideas and hypotheses may be the intended outcome of a discovery or brainstorming meeting.
